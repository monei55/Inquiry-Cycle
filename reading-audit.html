Build a picture of current reading practice

This audit is designed as a quick leadership scan, not a compliance checklist. Use more than one source of evidence and record what is actually visible in practice. The aim is to identify patterns that can inform the next phase of the Inquiry Cycle.

1. Use multiple evidence sources

Reading Signposts, walkthroughs, student data, student work, planning, staff voice and professional learning evidence.

2. Judge current practice

Rate each area as Not yet evident, Emerging, Consistent or Embedded, and record the evidence behind the judgement.

3. Identify the next focus

Look for patterns across the audit rather than selecting an area based on one observation or one data source.

Suggested evidence sources: Reading Signposts mapping, instructional walkthroughs, student reading data, student work, curriculum and planning documents, intervention records, staff capability information, student voice and leadership observations.

1. Reading Culture & Leadership

How visible, coherent and collectively owned is reading improvement across the school?

Reading is a clearly articulated school priority, understood by staff and connected to student outcomes.

Look for: shared language, improvement plans, meeting agendas, leadership communication, staff understanding.

Leaders regularly monitor reading practice and use evidence to guide support and next steps.

Look for: walkthrough schedules, feedback processes, leadership check-ins, review points, coaching or support.

2. Curriculum & Reading Demands

How deliberately are the reading demands of curriculum texts identified and planned for?

Planning identifies the reading demands students need to successfully access curriculum texts.

Look for: text complexity, vocabulary, background knowledge, text structure, comprehension demands and discipline-specific reading.

Students have regular access to appropriately challenging, purposeful and varied texts across learning areas.

Look for: classroom texts, planning, text selection, opportunities to read across genres and curriculum areas.

3. Explicit Reading Instruction

What do instructional walkthroughs show about the consistency and quality of reading instruction?

Walkthroughs show explicit teaching of the reading knowledge and strategies students need.

Look for: clear learning intentions, modelling, guided practice, checks for understanding, purposeful practice and feedback.

Students are supported to build accuracy, fluency, vocabulary, background knowledge and comprehension.

Look for: instructional routines, modelling, oral language, vocabulary instruction, repeated reading, comprehension discussion and knowledge building.

Students can explain what they are learning in reading, how they are improving and what successful reading looks like.

Look for: student voice during walkthroughs, learning walls, success criteria, feedback and student self-monitoring.

4. Assessment & Use of Evidence

How effectively is reading evidence used to identify need, plan instruction and monitor progress?

Multiple sources of reading evidence are used to identify strengths, needs and patterns.

Look for: screening, diagnostic data, student work, curriculum assessment, teacher observation and progress over time.

Reading data is interpreted and translated into instructional decisions rather than simply collected.

Look for: data discussions, regrouping, targeted teaching, progress monitoring, intervention decisions and changes to instruction.

5. Differentiation, Intervention & Extension

How well does the reading response match the different needs of learners?

Students requiring additional support are identified early and receive targeted instruction matched to need.

Look for: intervention entry criteria, diagnostic information, targeted goals, intensity, frequency and progress monitoring.

Classroom instruction is adjusted so all students can access appropriate challenge and support.

Look for: scaffolds, grouping, adjusted texts/tasks, extension, differentiated practice and evidence-informed teaching responses.

6. Staff Capability & Collaborative Practice

Do staff have the knowledge, confidence and support required to strengthen reading outcomes?

Staff share a common understanding of effective reading instruction and agreed school practices.

Look for: shared routines, common language, collaborative planning, moderation and consistency across classrooms.

Professional learning, coaching and support are responsive to identified staff and student needs.

Look for: staff capability surveys, professional learning plans, coaching, modelling, feedback and follow-up support.

Audit Summary & Next Steps

Use the patterns across the audit to identify where practice is strong, where evidence is inconsistent or missing, and which area may warrant deeper investigation through the Inquiry Cycle.
