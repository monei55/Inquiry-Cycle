Phase 1

Scan & Assess

Where are we now?

This phase develops a clear, evidence-based understanding of current practice and current outcomes. A strong scan moves beyond broad impressions and helps the team establish a shared view of current reality.

How it was used in the Reading CLC

1
Mapped current practice
Principals mapped their schools against the Reading Signposts.
2
Examined evidence
Current reading practices and student outcomes were discussed.
3
Identified patterns
Strengths, gaps and areas requiring further development were surfaced.

Guiding questions

What evidence do we have about current practice?

Consider observations, walkthroughs, planning documents, enacted curriculum, staff feedback and agreed instructional routines.

What evidence do we have about student outcomes?

Review multiple sources, including screening data, diagnostic information, student work, achievement data and progress over time.

What patterns or gaps are emerging?

Look for groups, cohorts, practices or curriculum areas where evidence consistently points to a need.

Leadership tip: Spend enough time understanding the problem before moving to a solution. A rushed scan often leads to a poorly targeted response.
Common pitfall: Relying on only one data source or using assumptions as evidence.

Phase 2

Prioritise & Focus

What is most important to focus on right now?

Schools often identify several areas that could be improved. This phase narrows the work to the priority most likely to make a meaningful difference and that can be influenced within the current context.

How it was used in the Reading CLC

1
Reviewed the scan
Evidence from the Reading Signposts and school discussions was considered.
2
Selected vocabulary
Vocabulary was identified as the shared area for improvement.
3
Deepened the focus
The group considered vocabulary demands within texts and staff capability needs.

Guiding questions

What does the evidence suggest is the most pressing need?

Identify the issue that appears most consistently and has a clear relationship to student learning.

Which area could have the greatest impact?

Consider leverage: strengthening one area may improve outcomes across several subjects or year levels.

What is within our sphere of influence?

Select a priority that the school can address through leadership, curriculum, teaching, support and monitoring.

Leadership tip: A clear focus creates coherence. It helps staff understand what matters, why it matters and where effort should be directed.
Common pitfall: Selecting too many priorities and spreading implementation effort too thinly.

Phase 3

Develop & Plan

What will we do in response?

This phase turns evidence into a coherent and targeted response. It clarifies actions, sequencing, roles, support structures and what successful implementation will look like.

How it was used in the Reading CLC

1
Audited staff capability
Staff confidence, prior professional learning and areas requiring support were identified.
2
Planned support
HOD-C support, DIBELS implementation and interpretation, and leadership-level support were considered.
3
Clarified responsibility
Roles, actions and support were aligned with the identified need.

Guiding questions

What knowledge or capability is needed?

Identify what staff and leaders need to know and be able to do in order to address the priority effectively.

What is already in place?

Build on current strengths, established routines, existing expertise and available resources.

How will we know implementation is occurring as intended?

Define observable indicators, agreed practices and evidence sources before implementation begins.

Leadership tip: A strong plan is more than a list of activities. It connects each action directly to the identified need.
Common pitfall: Planning professional learning without planning how it will transfer into classroom practice.

Phase 4

Take Action

How do we enact the plan in practice?

This phase translates the plan into professional learning, coaching, classroom support and leadership action. The emphasis is not simply on delivering an activity, but on supporting transfer into school-based practice.

How it was used in the Reading CLC

1
Delivered targeted learning
A Teams session focused on identifying vocabulary demands within texts.
2
Provided school-level support
HOD-C support helped staff apply learning in context.
3
Maintained leadership visibility
Leaders created the conditions required for implementation.

Guiding questions

What does implementation need to look like in our school?

Translate the shared plan into the routines, expectations and supports appropriate for the local context.

How will we support staff to apply the learning?

Use modelling, coaching, collaborative planning, observation, feedback and access to quality resources.

How will leaders keep the work visible?

Use meeting agendas, walkthroughs, check-ins, planning conversations and regular communication.

Leadership tip: Implementation improves when staff receive repeated opportunities to learn, practise, receive feedback and refine.
Common pitfall: Treating completion of professional development as evidence that practice has changed.

Phase 5

Monitor & Review

What impact is this having, and what do we need to do next?

Improvement is not measured by activity alone. This phase examines whether practice is changing, whether outcomes are improving and whether the current approach should continue, deepen, refine or change.

How it was used in the Reading CLC

1
Applied strategies
Schools began applying vocabulary strategies in their own contexts.
2
Monitored impact
Implementation, staff feedback, student work and assessment evidence were considered.
3
Repeated the cycle
Evidence informed whether to continue, refine or identify a new priority.

Guiding questions

What evidence do we have that practice is changing?

Use observations, planning artefacts, staff reflection, walkthrough evidence and consistency of agreed practices.

What evidence do we have that student outcomes are improving?

Compare relevant outcome measures over time and consider whether changes are evident for intended students and cohorts.

What should happen next?

Decide whether to continue and deepen, refine the current approach, or begin a new scan for the next priority.

Leadership tip: Schedule review points before implementation starts so monitoring does not become an afterthought.
Common pitfall: Using implementation activity as the only measure of success.
