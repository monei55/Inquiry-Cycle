The Inquiry Cycle

Phase 1

Scan & Assess

Develop a clear, evidence-based understanding of current practice and student outcomes.

Key question: Where are we now?

How we used this in the Reading CLC

  • Mapped schools against the Reading Signposts.
  • Considered where each school began and where it is now.
  • Examined current reading practices and student outcomes.
  • Identified strengths, patterns and areas for development.

Leadership tip

Do not rush to a solution. A strong scan draws on multiple sources of evidence and creates a shared understanding of current reality.

Questions for your school

What evidence do we have about current practice?

Consider walkthroughs, planning documents, classroom routines, staff feedback and curriculum artefacts.

What evidence do we have about student outcomes?

Consider screening data, classroom assessment, student work, achievement standards and progress over time.

What patterns or gaps are emerging?

Look across cohorts, year levels, student groups and teaching contexts rather than relying on a single result.

Ready to move on?

Phase 2

Prioritise & Focus

Select the highest-leverage area that is important, actionable and within your sphere of influence.

Key question: What is most important to focus on right now?

How we used this in the Reading CLC

  • Reviewed the evidence collected during the scan.
  • Identified vocabulary as the shared area for improvement.
  • Focused specifically on identifying vocabulary demands within texts.
  • Selected one area with potential to improve comprehension across contexts.

Common pitfall

Choosing several priorities at once can dilute attention, capability-building and monitoring. Focus creates depth.

Questions for your school

Which need is most strongly supported by the evidence?

Separate assumptions from evidence. Identify the issue that appears consistently across sources.

Which focus is most likely to improve outcomes?

Consider the potential impact on students, teaching practice and curriculum access.

What can we realistically influence and sustain?

A useful priority is significant enough to matter and focused enough to implement well.

Ready to move on?

Phase 3

Develop & Plan

Turn evidence into a coherent response with clear actions, responsibilities, support and measures of success.

Key question: What will we do in response?

How we used this in the Reading CLC

  • Used a staff confidence survey and professional learning audit.
  • Identified capability and support needs.
  • Planned HOD-C support for DIBELS implementation and interpretation.
  • Planned leadership-level support to unpack vocabulary demands within texts.

Leadership tip

A strong plan is more than a list of activities. It explains what will change, who will lead, how staff will be supported and what success will look like.

Questions for your school

What knowledge or capability is needed?

Identify what staff need to know, understand and be able to do differently.

What is already in place, and where are the gaps?

Build from current strengths rather than assuming the work must begin from scratch.

Who will lead, support and monitor each action?

Make roles, timelines, check-ins and expected evidence explicit.

Ready to move on?

Phase 4

Take Action

Translate the plan into professional learning, coaching, classroom practice and visible leadership action.

Key question: How do we enact the plan in practice?

How we used this in the Reading CLC

  • Delivered professional learning through the EFI SEOC team.
  • Built leadership understanding of vocabulary demands within texts.
  • Provided school-level support through HOD-C coaching and mentoring.
  • Supported leaders to transfer shared learning into school-based practice.

Common pitfall

Completing professional development is not the same as changing practice. Implementation requires follow-up, modelling, feedback and time.

Questions for your school

What should implementation look like in classrooms?

Describe the observable practices, routines and student experiences you expect to see.

How will staff be supported to apply the learning?

Consider modelling, co-planning, observation, coaching, feedback and collaborative review.

How will leaders keep the work visible?

Use agendas, walkthroughs, staff meetings, planning conversations and progress checks.

Ready to move on?

Phase 5

Monitor & Review

Examine whether agreed practices are being implemented and whether they are improving teaching and student outcomes.

Key questions: What impact is this having? What do we need to do next?

How this continues after the Reading CLC

  • Apply vocabulary strategies within school contexts.
  • Check whether agreed practices are being used.
  • Review staff feedback, student work and assessment evidence.
  • Continue, refine or select a new focus based on impact.

Leadership tip

Improvement is not measured by activity alone. Separate evidence of implementation from evidence of impact, then use both to decide what comes next.

Questions for your school

What evidence shows that practice is changing?

Look for consistency, quality, reach and staff confidence—not simply completion.

What evidence shows that students are benefiting?

Consider progress data, student work, engagement, transfer and achievement over time.

Should we continue, refine or begin a new focus?

Use the evidence to make a deliberate decision, then return to the relevant phase of the cycle.

Ready to begin the next cycle?
